# What this step is for

Ask why, repeatedly, against the point of cause Step 2 found — until you reach a systemic factor you can actually change, not a person you can blame.

## What good looks like

A root cause marked verified — confirmed against the actual process, not just reasoned about in a meeting room — that terminates on something systemic: a missing check, a worn tool, an unclear standard. Fishbone and 5-Why both belong here, not in Step 2.

## Common failure modes

- Stopping at "operator made a mistake." That's a symptom, not a root cause — ask why the mistake was possible.
- Never verifying the chain against the real process — a plausible-sounding 5-Why that nobody walked the floor to confirm.
- Doing root cause analysis on the whole problem instead of the specific point of cause Step 2 narrowed it to.

## Worked example

Why did the weld fail? → Current was out of spec. Why? → The welder's setting drifted. Why? → No periodic calibration check exists for that station. Verified by inspecting the maintenance log — no calibration record in 90 days. Root cause: no calibration standard for station 3.
