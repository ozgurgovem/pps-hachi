# What this step is for

Commit to a measurable target before looking for causes. This is the step that distinguishes the Toyota 8-step method from the 7-step Kaizen form the company already uses — and the one most often skipped in practice.

## What good looks like

A SMART target: metric, baseline, target value, unit, and a due date, all filled in. "Reduce weld rejects from 3.1% to 1.0% by week 34" — every field answerable, nothing implicit.

## Common failure modes

- Setting the target after the countermeasure is already known, so it just describes what you were going to do anyway.
- Leaving the due date or the baseline implicit — "get it back under control" has no date and no starting point.
- A target with no owner, so nobody is actually accountable when week 34 arrives.

## Worked example

Baseline 3.1% (week 26), target 1.0%, unit % rejects, due week 34, owner: line supervisor. Every field is a fact someone can check later, not a hope.
