# What this step is for

Choose what to change, and defend why this option beats the alternatives — every countermeasure traces back to a verified root cause from Step 4.

## What good looks like

Each countermeasure links to at least one verified root cause, and sits as high as practical in the error-proofing hierarchy — eliminate, prevent, detect — before falling back to training or a procedure change. If it does fall back to training only, the entry says why prevention wasn't feasible.

## Common failure modes

- A countermeasure with no link to a root cause — it might be a good idea, but this step isn't the place for good ideas in general.
- Reaching straight for "retrain the operator" when an error-proofing fixture was possible and nobody costed it out.
- Picking the first idea instead of comparing options — no record of what else was considered or why it lost.

## Worked example

Root cause: no calibration standard for station 3. Countermeasure: add a mandatory weekly calibration check with a signed log, plus a poka-yoke that blocks the weld cycle if calibration is overdue. Training alone was rejected — it doesn't prevent recurrence if someone forgets.
